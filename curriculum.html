Activity 01 · Elementary & seniors

The password lab

We teach that strong passwords are CLOUDS. Type something in and watch the six checks light up. The crack time assumes an attacker guessing four billion times a second — the same number we use in the assembly.

Time to crack — Waiting
·ComplexMixes upper case, lower case, numbers, and symbols.
·LengthyAt least 10 characters. A 6-character password falls in about three minutes.
·Only yoursNot your name, your birthday, or anything guessable from your profile.
·UniqueNot on the common-password lists. 91% of people pick from the top 1,000.
·DifferentA different password for every site, so one breach isn't all of them.
·Short termChanged every few months, and immediately after any breach.

This runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you type is sent anywhere or saved. Still — don't paste in a password you actually use. Habits matter more than tools.

Activity 02 · Elementary & seniors

Spot the phish

This is the message we put on screen mid-assembly. There are five things wrong with it. Tap each one you can find.

New message
From (Roblox Support)
To samskibidi@gmail.com (Sam Skibidi)
Subject

CLICK NOW TO CLAIM!!!

Terms and conditions apply.

0 of 5 red flags found
    That's all five. Too good to be true, all caps, a lookalike link, a request for personal info, and a spoofed sender. Any one of them is enough to delete the message — you don't need to find all five to be safe.

    Elementary assembly

    Intro to Cybersecurity

    Written for fourth and fifth graders and delivered as a full-class assembly. The run of show alternates explanation and competition — no segment runs long enough for a ten-year-old to check out.

    Run of show

    01 What & why
    Cybersecurity defined, then WannaCry, the CrowdStrike outage, and Stuxnet as proof it's not abstract.
    02 Passwords
    The CLOUDS rule, then rebuilding 1234 into something that takes centuries.
    03 Password game
    One student per class spins a wheel, draws cards, and builds a password. Longest crack time wins.
    04 CIA triad
    Confidentiality, integrity, availability — the three things security is actually protecting.
    05 How computers work
    Input, storage, memory, software, output. Plus a tour of Windows, macOS, and Linux.
    06 Threat vocabulary
    Threat, vulnerability, exploit, risk — and how they fit together.
    07 Social engineering
    Phishing, spear-phishing, vishing, smishing, and the live phish hunt above.
    08 Malware
    Viruses, worms, trojans, keyloggers, botnets, backdoors, logic bombs, and what antivirus does.
    09 ABCyber
    Whole-class game: spin a letter, name a cyber term starting with it, explain what it means.
    10 Cipher busters
    Decode a Morse message to close out.

    Malware, in kid-sized definitions

    Virus / worm
    Infects and spreads, with or without a human helping it along.
    Trojan horse
    A program with a hidden malicious function.
    Keylogger
    Records what you type to steal passwords and personal info.
    Botnet
    Compromised computers under someone else's control.
    Backdoor
    A way into a program that skips the normal security checks.
    Logic bomb
    Malware that waits, dormant, until a specific condition is met.

    Senior residences

    Train the staff, reach the residents.

    We're high schoolers with a Monday meeting — we can't be at a senior residence every week. So the program is built to outlast our visit.

    We run an Intro to Cybersecurity workshop adapted for staff who support senior residents, and leave behind a Teaching Cyber Safety Instructional Guide written so a caregiver can walk a resident through a scam call or a suspicious text without needing us in the room. That's how 45 trained staff turn into more than 800 residents reached.

    What the guide covers

    Scam defense
    Recognizing phishing emails, text scams, and phone fraud with real examples.
    Passwords
    The same CLOUDS rule, paced for people who'd rather write it down than memorize it.
    Device help
    Updates, account recovery, and privacy settings, step by step.
    What to do next
    Who to call, what to freeze, and how to report it after something goes wrong.

    Coming this year: a dedicated digital literacy curriculum alongside cyber safety, plus booths and short sessions at senior community events.
