About

A club that turned into an obligation.

CyberCEO started in Summer 2023 as a handful of Del Norte students who thought cybersecurity was interesting and noticed nobody was teaching it to the people most likely to get hit. Three years later we're still students — and there are now more than two thousand people who've sat through one of our workshops.

Mission

Spread awareness and educate the broader community about cyber and information security.

Not a slogan — it's the sentence we test every new program against. If a lesson doesn't change what someone does the next time they get a suspicious text, it isn't ready.

Vision

Every school and community center in the county has someone who knows what to do.

We can't be in every room. So we train the adults who already are — teachers, library staff, senior residence caregivers — and hand them materials they can keep using without us.

Timeline

How we got here.

Each phase added an audience we hadn't figured out how to reach yet.

Summer 2023

Founded

A student pitch — Ethan Zhao, Risha Guha, Advik Garg, and Will Cheng — to build a stable organization, run workshops for elementary schoolers, and develop training for senior citizens.

2023 / 24

Building the curriculum

Foundation year: recruiting, subteams, and writing the Intro to Cybersecurity deck from scratch — passwords, phishing, malware, the CIA triad — pitched at a fourth grade reading level without being condescending.

2024 / 25

Into the classrooms

Assemblies across six Poway Unified elementary campuses, eventually reaching 48 fourth and fifth grade classes. In parallel, a separate workshop deck for senior residence staff and the Teaching Cyber Safety Instructional Guide.

Summer 2025

Beyond school walls

A free open workshop at 4S Ranch Library, and a summer camp that pulled participants from outside California entirely — the first sign the material travels.

2025 / 26

Diversify

The Cybersecurity Expert Panel brings working professionals to Del Norte students, and the year's theme pushes every program to reach an audience it hasn't before.

2025 / 26 direction

What "diversify" actually means.

Schools

  • Reach past Poway Unified elementary: Rowe Elementary in September, plus middle schools and private schools.
  • More workshops in lower-income areas.
  • Rework lesson depth and offer more formats — pamphlets, a book, self-paced courses.
  • Condense the summer camp material for shorter sessions.

Senior communities

  • Teach at senior community events with booths and lightning sessions.
  • Add a dedicated digital literacy curriculum alongside cyber safety.
  • Get the Teaching Cyber Safety handbook into more hands.

Visibility and funding

  • Launch this website.
  • Grow social media with reels, an activity of the month, and event coverage.
  • Pursue corporate sponsorships and matching gifts.
  • Run student fundraisers around Club Rush.

Member experience

  • Tour the City of San Diego's Cyber Hub.
  • Build cyber games and actually play them at meetings.
  • Host an annual speaker event for the wider school.
  • Collaborate with other DNHS clubs on joint events.

Get involved

If you run a room full of people, we'd like to visit it.

Classrooms, libraries, community centers, senior residences — the workshop is free and adapts to the audience. Tell us who we'd be talking to and how long we have.
