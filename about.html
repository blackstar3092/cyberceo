About

A student-run nonprofit teaching cybersecurity across San Diego County.

CyberCEO started in Summer 2023 as a group of Del Norte students who wanted to teach cyber and information security to people who were not getting it anywhere else. Since then we have run workshops for more than 2,000 students, trained staff at two senior residences, and hosted an expert panel at our own school. Everything we run is free.

Mission

Spread awareness and educate the broader community about cyber and information security.

This is the sentence we test every new program against. If a lesson does not change what someone does the next time they get a suspicious text, it is not ready.

Vision

Every school and community center in the county has someone who knows what to do.

We cannot be in every room, so we train the adults who already are: teachers, library staff, and senior residence caretakers. Then we hand them materials they can keep using without us.

Timeline

How we got here

Each year added an audience we had not figured out how to reach yet.

Summer 2023

Founded

A student pitch by Ethan Zhao, Risha Guha, Advik Garg, and Will Cheng to build a stable organization, run workshops for elementary schoolers, and develop training for senior citizens.

2023 / 24

Building the curriculum

Recruiting, setting up subteams, and writing the Introduction to Cybersecurity deck from scratch. Passwords, phishing, malware, and the CIA triad, written for a fourth grade audience without talking down to them.

2024 / 25

Into the classrooms

Assemblies across six Poway Unified elementary campuses, eventually covering 48 fourth and fifth grade classes. At the same time we built a separate workshop for senior residence staff and wrote the Teaching Cyber Safety Instructional Guide.

Summer 2025

Beyond school walls

A free open workshop at 4S Ranch Library, and a summer camp that drew participants from outside California. The first sign the material travels.

2025 / 26

Diversify

We hosted our first Cybersecurity Expert Panel at Del Norte, ran our first assembly outside Poway Unified at Rowe Elementary, added new lesson formats, and started teaching at senior community events rather than waiting for bookings.

2026 / 27

Expansion

More senior residences, more districts, and a second expert panel. The programs are built. This year is about running them in more places.

2026 / 27 direction

What expansion looks like

Senior communities

  • Return to Villa Rancho Bernardo and add homes such as Remington House.
  • Run staff training and leave the caretaker guide at every new residence.
  • Keep teaching at senior community events with booths and short sessions.
  • Continue building out digital literacy material alongside cyber safety.

Schools

  • Reach elementary schools in districts outside Poway Unified.
  • Prioritize schools that have no cybersecurity programming at all.
  • Extend into middle schools and private schools.
  • Offer the shorter, condensed version of the lesson where a full assembly will not fit.

Events and partnerships

  • Host a second Cybersecurity Expert Panel at Del Norte.
  • Run library workshops and summer camps open to anyone.
  • Bring staff training to local schools, not just senior residences.
  • Build relationships with cybersecurity companies for speakers and sponsorship.

The club itself

  • Grow enrollment and get more members presenting, not just planning.
  • Keep developing cyber games to use at meetings and in lessons.
  • Pursue corporate sponsorships and employer matching.
  • Collaborate with other Del Norte clubs on joint events.
